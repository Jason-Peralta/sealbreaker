# Spike S1 — Combat feel: server-authoritative swings with an arc hit test

Date: 2026-09-05 · Status: **part A answered yes, part B answered "yes with limits", part C open** · Code: `sb_combat` module (kept; it is the seed of the real combat core, not throwaway)

## Question

Can we drive a weapon swing from a server-side swing state, hit everything in an arc during an active window, and have the player's animation follow that timing, without external libraries and without Mixins?

## What was built

## Answers

**A. Server-authoritative swing + arc hit test: yes.** The whole loop (request, validate, attach state, sync, tick, arc test, hit once, feedback) is about 250 lines and uses only NeoForge attachments, payloads and events. Numbers are in JSON and reload with the world.

**B. Third-person animation driven by our timing: yes, with a limit.** NeoForge's `RegisterRenderStateModifiersEvent` (`registerAvatarEntityModifier`) lets us set `attackTime` and `attackArm` on the avatar's render state every frame, so the vanilla attack *pose* plays over our windup → active → recovery timeline instead of vanilla's fixed 6 ticks. The limit: the pose shape is vanilla's. A *different* pose per move (a thrust, an overhead, a plunge) needs one of: (1) a Mixin at the tail of `HumanoidModel#setupAnim` to apply our keyframes after vanilla resets the pose, (2) rendering our own animated copy of the arm in a render layer while hiding vanilla's, or (3) PlayerAnimator if it reaches 26.x (allowed as a utility). Recommendation for Milestone 1: option 1, one Mixin in `sb_core`, justified in the decision log, applying keyframes exported from Blockbench through the vanilla `KeyframeAnimation` system.

**C. First person: open.** The spike triggers the vanilla first-person swing once per swing; it is shorter than our timeline and always the same shape. `RenderArmEvent` and `RenderHandEvent` exist and expose the pose stack and arm model part, so per-move first-person animation is feasible without Mixins; it is the fiddly part the PRD already flagged and gets its own week in Milestone 1.

## Things learned that change the plan

- **Multi-module runs work** the way the roadmap assumed: `sb_combat`'s run configurations load `sb_core` from its source set (`mods { sb_core { sourceSet(project(':sb_core').sourceSets.main) } }`) with `compileOnly project(':sb_core')` for compilation. `runGameTestServer` from `sb_combat` runs every module's tests.
- **`minecraft:empty` is a 1×1×1 test template** and entities spawned outside it may sit in chunks the entity query cannot see; the game test now checks geometry per entity. Milestone 1 chore: author our own test template with a platform so tests can use the real entity query.
- **API renames on 26.2 worth remembering:** `EntityTypes.PIG` (constants moved out of `EntityType`), `ClientPacketDistributor.sendToServer`, `DeferredRegister.createDataComponents(Registries.DATA_COMPONENT_TYPE, modid)`, `Identifier` instead of `ResourceLocation`, players are `Avatar`s with `AvatarRenderState`. The unobfuscated source jar under `build/moddev/artifacts/` answers these in seconds.
- **Hold-to-attack falls out of the input event**: NeoForge re-fires the attack event every tick while the button is held on a block, so holding chains swings near blocks but not in open air. Milestone 1's input resolution (tap / hold / airborne / sprint) must read the key state directly instead of relying on this event alone.
- **JBR hot-swap flags** are now on every `sb_combat` run (`-XX:+IgnoreUnrecognizedVMOptions -XX:+AllowEnhancedClassRedefinition`), harmless on a normal JDK; selecting JBR 25.0.2 (bundled with IntelliJ 2026.1.1) as the run JRE turns them on.

## Update, 5 Sep 2026 (after the first playtest)

Feedback: the swing was too slow and the vanilla pose did not match the hitbox. Changes, all verified by the unit tests, the headless game test and a clean client launch:

- **Three-move tap combo, data-driven** (`docs/data/weapon_archetype.md`): sweep left-to-right (0.7x), sweep right-to-left (0.7x), overhead (1.4x, 50 degree cone, longer recovery). A tap during recovery cancels into the next move; a tap during wind-up or active is buffered; a tap within 12 ticks after a move continues the combo; later taps restart. Moves are 8, 8 and 11 ticks instead of the earlier 14.
- **The hit region follows the blade.** A sweep divides its arc into one window per active tick and walks it from the starting side to the other, so a left-to-right sweep reaches a target on the left first and one on the right last; the game test asserts exactly that with pigs at plus and minus 60 degrees.
- **Part B answered properly: custom third-person poses through decision 0017's Mixin.** `HumanoidPoseHooks` in core runs at the tail of `HumanoidModel#setupAnim`; `sb_combat` registers a poser that cocks the arm and twists the torso to the starting side during wind-up, sweeps across during the active window, and settles during recovery; the overhead raises the arm above the head and chops. The vanilla attack pose is suppressed while a move plays.
- **Part C answered for the item: first-person animation through `RenderHandEvent`.** The vanilla hand render is cancelled during a move and the held item is drawn with the move's own transform: a horizontal flat-blade cut across the screen for the sweeps, a raise-and-chop for the overhead. The vanilla first-person arm mesh is not drawn during our moves; drawing our own arm is a Milestone 1 polish item.
- **Poses are procedural constants for now** (`SwingAnimations`); the phase structure (wind-up, active, recovery per move) is what Blockbench keyframes will slot into.

## Update, 5 Sep 2026 (second pass: "look at someone swinging a sword")

Feedback on the procedural poses: rough even for a proof of concept. The second pass replaced them with a keyframe pipeline and authored the three cuts against real sword mechanics (wind-up, a cut whose peak speed is at the centre, follow-through past the centre, recovery) and against how Better Combat and PlayerAnimator do it (one animation per attack with an upswing, the hitbox timed to it; the first-person view rendered from the same animation).

What changed, all verified by 9 unit tests, the 3 headless game tests and screenshot review:

- **Keyframe animations in data** (`docs/data/weapon_archetype.md`): `assets/<ns>/sb_animations/player/*.json` in the Bedrock/Blockbench export shape (bones, rotation and position channels, easing, catmull-rom), hot-reloadable, referenced by a move's `animation` field. The sword's cuts are generated by `tools/gen_sword_anim.py` until the team authors in Blockbench.
- **A rig, not a part list.** `AnimationPoser` rotates the `body` about the hips and carries the arms and head with it (composing the rotations and moving the pivots), so a torso twist drives a cut the way it does for a person. `right_item` is a wrist bone pivoting at the hand: 90° lays the blade along the forearm, y twists the flat toward the camera (a sprite is invisible edge-on), z cocks the blade so it lags and whips through a horizontal cut. `HeldItemPose` is shared by the third-person item layer and the first-person renderer, so the blade is placed identically in both.
- **First person draws the real arm** (`FirstPersonArmRenderer`): the player's own arm mesh hangs from a shoulder placed relative to the eyes (pushed 0.65 blocks forward and 0.05 up for readability; at the true position the near plane cut the upper arm to a stump), posed by the same `body`, `right_arm` and `right_item` tracks plus vanilla's held-item raise. One animation serves both views, and what the player sees is the path the hit test sweeps.
- **Blending between combo moves** (`SwingBlendTracker`, `BlendedPose`): when the next move interrupts a recovery, the interrupted pose fades into the new one over 3 ticks instead of snapping to rest.
- **A contact-sheet debugger** (`AnimDebug`, run `runClientAnim`): joins the quick-play world, freezes each animation every 0.05 s and screenshots it from behind, at three-quarters, from the side, from the front and in first person, then quits. Every convention above was established by reading probe poses off those sheets, not by derivation; the derivations were right but a bug in the wrist pivot and the near-plane stump made two earlier readings wrong. The workflow is in `docs/data/weapon_archetype.md`.
- **The cuts.** Left-to-right (a backhand): torso winds 38° left with the arm across the body high and the blade cocked behind the hand, then unwinds through the front between 0.25 and 0.35 s (the active ticks), level at contact, and follows through low and 34° right, ending where the right-to-left forehand chambers. Right-to-left is the mirror. Reviewed from behind, the side, the front and in first person: the blade crosses the front inside the active window.
- **Swipe, swipe, thrust** (user direction after the first sheets): the third move is a thrust, not an overhead. New `shape: thrust` in the archetype (a narrow cone, 30°, reach 3.4, 5/2/6 ticks, 1.4×, knockback 0.7); the animation draws the shoulder back and the hand to the hip with the blade level and forward, then turns the shoulder in and drives the arm out level with a lunge, the tip landing on the crosshair in first person inside 0.25–0.35 s.
- **Edge leads.** The wrist turns the flat level (`right_item` y 80) through both sweeps, so from any third-person angle the cut shows the edge and the tip travelling, not the flat slapping; the thrust keeps the flat vertical in a hammer grip. The sprite is nearly invisible edge-on, so 10° of tilt toward the first-person camera is kept.
- **Held in the fist** (user feedback: "the sword is just on top of the hand"). Vanilla attaches a handheld item at the very end of the arm, so the handle's middle sits on the fist's bottom edge, and laying the blade along the forearm buried the handle inside the arm. Measured from the sword sprite (the handle is 5.4 scaled pixels below the sprite centre along the blade; vanilla puts that centre 4 pixels past the grip point), the grip now sits two pixels up the arm so the handle's middle is in the centre of the fist with the pommel a pixel out of its back, and the wrist pivots there. The cuts keep the blade 35–55° off the forearm in the horizontal plane (lagging at the chamber, leading through contact and the follow-through) and the thrust keeps it 20° above the forearm with the arm driven 20° below level, as a real point-forward grip does. So that nothing hops, a weapon in hand is now always drawn through this grip, in a data-driven idle stance (`idle` on the archetype: arm up a little, blade forward-up) that moves blend out of and back into.

## Update, 5 Sep 2026 (third pass, after the user played it)

Feedback: the first person looked like "a hand holding a sword" (a giant arm block), the thrust was far too fast and did not read as a thrust, the combo could be rushed, and the thrust and the swings should follow where the player looks. Changes, all verified by 9 unit tests, the 3 headless game tests (one new assertion) and contact sheets from five angles:

- **First person is vanilla's framing again**, driven by the rig: the weapon alone, exactly where vanilla draws a held item when idle, oriented by the same fist frame the third-person rig computes (so a level blade is a level blade, and the thrust drives at the crosshair) and moved by the fist's travel from the idle stance, damped sideways so a cut stays on screen and exaggerated forward so a thrust visibly recedes. No arm at all: a real arm 0.7 blocks from the camera fills the screen, and vanilla shows none with an item either. The real-arm renderer is gone.
- **Moves play out in full.** A tap during a move is buffered and the next move starts the tick the current one ends; recovery is no longer cancellable. Because of that the three animations are authored as one chained sequence, the way a real combo flows: the backhand ends on its follow-through, which is the forehand's chamber; the forehand ends on the left, which is where the thrust withdraws from; the thrust ends on the idle stance. The client fades whatever is on screen into a move's first frame (3 ticks) and a finished move into the idle (6 ticks), so nothing snaps whether the combo continues or stops.
- **The thrust is a thrust**: 18 ticks (0.90 s) instead of 13. Withdraw to the hip with the point kept on line (0.22 s), coil the shoulder, drive the arm out level over 0.20 s with a lunge, hold at full extension through a 3-tick hit window (0.40–0.55 s), withdraw over 0.35 s. At full extension the shoulder turns in 12° and the arm angles 8° out so the blade points down the look line.
- **Swings follow the look.** During a move the body turns to the look yaw at half the difference per tick (vanilla does this during its own attack animation; ours never ran it), the torso takes on 70% of the look pitch in third person (the head keeps its own pitch), and the hit region's height window is centred on the look line at the target's distance, so a thrust aimed up at a target four blocks up reaches it and a level one does not (game test).
- Reference for the mechanics: one-handed cuts are driven from the hips, the arm extends through the cut and the follow-through of one cut is the chamber of the next (a moulinet), which is why the chained authoring; a thrust keeps the point on line and extends the arm before the body arrives (hand before foot), withdrawing faster than it went out.

## Update, 5 Sep 2026 (fourth pass: polish, "a video game feel")

Feedback: weightless, no polish; the first swing looked like the sword went through the player's own neck. Tricks taken from how combat mods and action games sell a hit, each verified by playing the real combo through the server in the debug world (`-Psb.animdebug=combo:fp` / `combo:back` screenshots every other tick):

- **Anticipation, snap, overshoot, settle.** Each cut now coils a touch deeper and holds for a beat, then snaps from chamber to contact in three ticks (ease-in) and decelerates through the follow-through (ease-out), overshooting the end pose and settling back onto it. The thrust drives to full extension in a tenth of a second, overshoots a hair, hard-stops and holds through its hit window, then withdraws.
- **Impact frames (hitstop).** A move's first hit shifts its server timeline by `hitstop_ticks` (2 for the sweeps, 3 for the thrust); every client freezes the attacker's pose over the gap and the attacker's camera kicks (a decaying pitch and roll over 5 ticks). This is the fighting-game trick that makes a hit feel like it connected with something.
- **Camera bone.** Animations carry a `camera` bone the local player's view follows: the head sways 1–2° with each cut and dips 2° into the lunge.
- **Blade arc.** An additive ribbon between where the blade was over the last six ticks and where it is, sampled from the rig in world space for every avatar mid-strike and drawn in the level's custom-geometry pass, so it shows in first person along the hit test's path.
- **Sound on the strike.** The whoosh plays the tick the hit window opens (as Better Combat does), not at the wind-up; the thrust is lower and louder; the hit sound drops in pitch for the thrust.
- **First-person guards.** The hand never comes nearer than arm's length, a blade that would point behind the camera plane is turned to point along the screen, and the flat is twisted to face a point above the viewer, which is where the level's light comes from. That is what removed the "through my own neck" chamber and the black-slab frames.

## Update, 5 Sep 2026 (fifth pass: the cuts themselves)

User verdict on the fourth pass: the arc, the sound and the impact frames are right; the sway is a third too much; the animation itself is lifeless and "the sword looks like you're just holding it out at a strange angle"; the arc must follow the swing closely so the player reads the hit window from it.

- **Diagonal cuts, like the combat mods and like a real one-handed cut.** The flat horizontal sweeps are gone. Each cut is raised over a shoulder with the blade back, the way vanilla's own grip holds a sword, whips over the top and across the front at chest height with the wrist opening so the blade runs out along the forearm, and finishes low on the far side. The backhand rises over the left shoulder and ends low right; the forehand rises from there over the right shoulder and ends low left; the thrust withdraws from there. On this blocky model a horizontal sweep reads as holding the sword out sideways; a diagonal reads as a cut from behind, from the side and in first person, and its arc is a big swoosh that shows the hit window. Reviewed by playing the real combo in the debug world from all three views.
- **Idle is vanilla's hold, lifted a little**, not a raised ready stance, so between moves the sword looks like a Minecraft sword in hand.
- **The arc follows what the viewer sees.** For the local player in first person the ribbon is sampled from the drawn weapon (its hand transform already holds the inverse view rotation, so the camera position plus the transformed point is the world position); other avatars keep the rig's world-space blade. First-person travel across the screen is no longer damped, so a cut crosses from one side to the other.
- **Sway** scaled to 65%.

## Update, 5 Sep 2026 (sixth pass: the crosshair)

User verdict on the fifth pass: the forehand is right; the thrust goes off to the bottom right, the backhand looks aimed at the top right of the screen, and every swing must pass through the crosshair or aiming is a pain; the idle should stay faithful to vanilla.

- **Framing rule.** First person now frames every move around the crosshair by construction: the pose at the move's contact moment is placed once, the blade's reference point measured (the tip of a thrust, the middle of a cut), and the move shifted so that point sits on the crosshair at contact, the shift ramping in over the wind-up so the wind-up starts where the hand really is. A thrust is additionally turned to point straight down the view line around its hit window. Verified by playing the combo: the backhand now rises upper-centre and crosses the crosshair on its way down to the right, the forehand rises upper-right and crosses it down to the left, the thrust lands its tip on it.
- **Idle** is vanilla's own hold with an 18° lean toward the middle of the screen; the raise in the rig's idle is gone.
- Camera sway at 65%.

Still open: feel in a person's hands (timing, arcs and every angle are in the two JSON files without touching Java); a hit-confirm flash or particle burst at the blade rather than at the target; Blockbench authoring instead of the generator script; an overhead chop animation for weapons that want one (the shape is still supported). Next per the user: the jump attack, the dash attack and the charge attack.


## Update, 8 Sep 2026 (#16: airborne, sprint and charged sword moves)

The sword now has all four contexts. `src/data/sword.json` is the authored balance source, validated and emitted by combat datagen; `tools/gen_sword_anim.py` emits the three new clips alongside the tap combo. The air move raises the sword then points it down, keeps the downward cone active until landing, and uses the synced landing timestamp for the impact pause and recovery. Sprint applies one data-driven forward impulse and reaches half a block beyond the first tap. Holding winds up an overhead heavy cut; release opens its hit window with the server's charge multiplier and a camera dip. A plunge's third-person pose stays aligned with gravity even when the player looks up or down.

The frame debugger produced 245 frames covering back, quarter, side, front and first-person views. Reviewed wind-up, contact, follow-through and recovery samples for all three moves. This caught a first-person recovery fault: the contact-centering offset stayed applied at the final idle keyframe. It now fades during recovery so the weapon returns to its idle framing. Third-person samples show the raised anticipation, downward plunge, cross-body lunge and overhead heavy; first-person contact remains visible. These are an engineering visual review, not the player's verdict on feel.

Two actual clients joined a LAN-published integrated server on this Mac (Java 25, Apple M3 Pro). Both observed the same `startTick` for tap (2837), sprint (2917), charge anticipation (2997), heavy release (3020, damage scale 2) and air (3077). Tap/sprint/air were observed at the same world tick; heavy release arrived at 3028 on the guest and was captured at 3029 on the host. Both rendered the host's sword and charge pose. Screenshot requests run on client ticks and can capture the preceding rendered frame, so these still images are not frame-identical synchronization measurements. The run used real request payloads and attachment sync, with staged positions/movement; it did not exercise keyboard input or a dedicated server.

The real buffered tap combo also ran through the server in first person (`combo:fp`, 36 frames); the reviewed contact sheet shows both cuts, the thrust, trails and chaining.

Reproduction: `runClientSwordFrames` for the five views; add `-Psb.animdebug=combo:fp` for the real buffered combo; `runClientSwordHost` then `runClientSwordGuest` for the network path. Each host/frame run creates its own fresh world and builds a lit stage at loaded spawn. Early captures with an unloaded origin, a hidden first-person weapon, or a paused menu were rejected and rerun; they do not count as acceptance. See `docs/data/weapon_archetype.md` for the commands and schema.

Automated coverage reads the shipped sword table, validates all contexts, downward target selection and real damage, the once-only lunge impulse, capped charge, continued air activity and landing recovery. JUnit also checks blade direction for both hands and exact elapsed ticks in old worlds. Still required: human feel/animation sign-off and the M1 dedicated-server/AC-08 acceptance run; no blind playtest or dedicated-server pass is claimed.
